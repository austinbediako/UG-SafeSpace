# SafeSpace UG — Platform Overview & Walkthrough

## About This Document

This document provides a comprehensive visual walkthrough of the **SafeSpace UG** platform — a digital ecosystem purpose-built to support the University of Ghana's commitment to addressing sexual harassment and misconduct within its community. It is intended for university leadership, policy teams, institutional reviewers, and external evaluators who wish to understand the platform's purpose, design philosophy, and user experience without requiring technical background.

Every section includes annotated screenshots and detailed explanations of what each interface achieves, why it was designed the way it was, and how it contributes to the broader institutional mission of safety, fairness, confidentiality, and accountability.

---

## 1. Platform Vision & Purpose

Sexual harassment and misconduct in academic institutions demand a response that is simultaneously compassionate and procedurally rigorous. The University of Ghana's **2017 Policy on Sexual Harassment and Misconduct** established clear definitions, reporting structures, investigation timelines, and disciplinary frameworks. However, translating a written policy into lived institutional practice requires more than awareness campaigns and printed guidelines.

**SafeSpace UG** exists to bridge that gap.

The platform digitises the full lifecycle of the university's harassment response framework — from the moment a community member first learns about their rights, through the act of filing a report, to the structured investigation and resolution process managed by the institution's designated committee. Rather than consolidating all of these responsibilities into a single application, SafeSpace UG intentionally separates them into distinct, purpose-built portals, each designed around the emotional and procedural needs of its specific audience.

**The platform is organised into four interconnected applications:**

1. **The Public Awareness Platform** — An open educational resource that helps community members understand what constitutes harassment, what their rights are, and how the reporting process works, before they ever need to use it.

2. **The Reporting Portal** — A secure, guided intake system where individuals can file formal or informal complaints, with full support for anonymous submission.

3. **The Participation Portal** — A private, role-based workspace where complainants, respondents, and witnesses can track their involvement in active cases, view deadlines, attend hearings, and access support resources.

4. **The Committee Dashboard** — An administrative operations centre where designated committee members coordinate investigations, manage hearings, enforce institutional deadlines, and maintain a transparent record of all actions taken.

This separation is deliberate. A person seeking to understand their rights should never be confronted with administrative complexity. A respondent should never have access to a complainant's private correspondence. A committee member should have full operational visibility without compromising the confidentiality of individual participants. Each application serves its audience with clarity and care.

## 2. The Public Awareness Platform

### Purpose & Philosophy

The Public Awareness Platform is the front door of the SafeSpace UG ecosystem. It exists for a simple but critically important reason: **people cannot exercise rights they do not understand.**

Before any reporting takes place, before any investigation begins, there must be a space where students, faculty, and staff can learn — without pressure, without login requirements, and without any record of their visit — what sexual harassment means under university policy, what protections exist for those who come forward, and what the institutional process looks like from beginning to end.

This platform is entirely public. No account is needed. No personal information is collected. The design philosophy is rooted in accessibility and reassurance: warm colours, clear typography, intuitive navigation, and language that transforms dense legal text into something a first-year student can understand on their first read.

### Homepage — The First Impression

The homepage was crafted to balance institutional authority with human warmth. Rather than opening with legal definitions or bureaucratic language, it welcomes visitors with clear messaging about the university's commitment to a safe community. As the user scrolls, the page progressively introduces key concepts through smooth, gentle animations that reveal content at a natural reading pace.

The visual hierarchy is intentional. The most important message — that help is available and reporting is safe — appears immediately. Supporting information about definitions, rights, and procedures unfolds naturally as the user continues exploring. This scroll-based revelation prevents information overload and allows users to absorb difficult subject matter at their own pace.

The opening section establishes trust and institutional commitment. The design avoids clinical or legalistic tones, instead using approachable language and visual warmth to signal that this is a safe space for learning.

As the user scrolls, additional sections reveal key information about definitions, rights, and available support. Each section is visually distinct, helping users locate exactly the information they need without feeling overwhelmed by a wall of text.

### Policy Summary — Making Legal Language Accessible

The University of Ghana's 2017 policy document is comprehensive but dense. The Policy Summary page exists to translate that document into clear, readable language that any member of the university community can understand. Key definitions, prohibited behaviours, and institutional responsibilities are presented in structured sections with plain-language explanations.

This page answers the question every potential reporter asks first: *"Does what happened to me count?"* By providing clear, accessible definitions alongside real-world examples, the platform removes ambiguity and empowers individuals to make informed decisions about their next steps.

The Reporting Guide addresses this directly by walking users through the entire institutional process step by step — from initial complaint submission through the 60-day investigation window, to the final resolution. The Your Rights page complements this by explicitly outlining the protections afforded to every participant in the process: the right to representation, protection against retaliation, the right to be informed of progress, and the right to appeal outcomes. The Support Resources page connects users to counselling services, legal aid, peer support networks, and emergency contacts. This page recognises that the platform is not a replacement for human support — it is a bridge to it.

## 3. The Reporting Portal

### Purpose & Philosophy

The Reporting Portal is where awareness transitions into action. This is the application that handles the formal intake of complaints — the moment when a community member decides to come forward and initiate the institutional response process.

The design of this portal is guided by a single principle: **the act of reporting should never feel more difficult than the experience being reported.** Every element of the interface — from the language used in form labels to the order in which questions are presented — has been considered through the lens of someone who may be experiencing fear, shame, confusion, or urgency.

### The Intake Experience

The reporting process is structured as a guided flow rather than a single overwhelming form. Users are walked through their submission step by step, beginning with the type of report they wish to file (formal or informal), then progressing through the details of the incident, identification of involved parties, and the option to upload supporting evidence.

Critically, the portal provides full support for **anonymous submission**. When a user chooses to report anonymously, their identity is not linked to the submission in any way. Instead, a secure Case Reference Number is generated that allows them to track the status of their report without ever revealing who they are. This feature exists because the university recognises that some individuals are not yet ready to identify themselves — but their experiences still deserve to be heard and addressed.

The interface is deliberately calm and uncluttered. There are no distracting elements, no unnecessary graphics, and no pressure to complete the form in a single session. The design communicates patience: *take your time, we are here when you are ready.*

![Reporting Home](./screenshots/phase2_home.png)

> It is worth noting that the institutional response process formally begins once a report is successfully submitted. Under the university's policy framework, the designated committee must acknowledge receipt and notify relevant parties within defined timeframes. The platform tracks these deadlines automatically, ensuring that no report falls through administrative gaps.

## 4. Identity & Access — How Users Are Verified

### Purpose & Philosophy

SafeSpace UG handles deeply sensitive matters — allegations of misconduct, personal testimony, evidence submissions, and institutional decisions that can affect careers, academic standing, and lives. For this reason, **the platform does not allow open public registration.** There is no "Sign Up" button that anyone on the internet can click to create an account.

This is an intentional and fundamental design decision rooted in institutional integrity.

### Why Access Is Institutionally Governed

The platform is designed to operate within the University of Ghana's existing identity ecosystem. Every student, faculty member, and staff member at the university already possesses institutional credentials — a university ID number, an institutional email address, and a verified role within the university community. SafeSpace UG leverages this existing identity infrastructure rather than creating a parallel one.

This approach serves several critical purposes:

**Preventing Impersonation.** In a system that handles misconduct allegations, it is essential that every participant is who they claim to be. By tying access to verified institutional identities, the platform eliminates the risk of external actors creating fraudulent accounts to file false reports, interfere with ongoing cases, or access sensitive information.

**Ensuring Accountability.** Every action taken within the platform — from filing a complaint to rendering a committee decision — is attributable to a verified member of the university community. This accountability is foundational to the integrity of the process.

**Preserving Confidentiality.** By restricting access to verified university members, the platform ensures that sensitive case information is only accessible to individuals with a legitimate institutional relationship to the matter at hand.

**Supporting Institutional Governance.** The university's harassment policy designates specific roles and responsibilities — committee members, investigators, designated support personnel. The authentication system ensures that only individuals properly assigned to these roles can access the corresponding administrative functions.

In a production deployment, the authentication system would integrate directly with the university's official identity management infrastructure, ensuring seamless verification against current enrolment and employment records. Users do not create new accounts — they access the platform using their existing institutional credentials.

### The Login Experience

The authentication interface is clean and purposeful. Users identify themselves using their university ID or institutional email address along with a personal passcode. The interface provides clear pathways for password recovery, ensuring that access barriers are minimal for legitimate users while remaining robust against unauthorised access.

![Auth Login](./screenshots/phase3_login.png)

The registration view exists to support the initial onboarding of verified users into the platform — not as an open public registration form. Access is always tied to institutional identity verification.

![Auth Register](./screenshots/phase3_register.png)

The password recovery flow provides a straightforward pathway for users who need to regain access to their accounts, ensuring that temporary credential issues never prevent someone from participating in time-sensitive case activities.

## 5. The Participation Portal

### Purpose & Philosophy

The Participation Portal is perhaps the most emotionally significant application in the SafeSpace UG ecosystem. This is where individuals who are directly involved in an active case — whether as a complainant, a respondent, or a witness — come to manage their participation in the institutional process.

The design challenge here is profound: **how do you create an interface that serves people on opposite sides of an allegation with equal fairness, clarity, and respect?**

The answer lies in role-based visibility. Every user who logs into the Participation Portal sees only the information that is relevant to their specific role in their specific case. A complainant sees their own submissions, their hearing schedule, and their correspondence with the committee. A respondent sees the allegations made against them, the deadline for their formal response, and their own hearing schedule. A witness sees only the specific matter they have been asked to contribute to.

No participant can see another participant's private information, communications, or case materials. This strict separation protects everyone involved and ensures that the platform itself cannot become a vector for intimidation, harassment, or unfair advantage.

### The Dashboard — A Calm Centre in a Difficult Process

When a participant first logs into the portal, the dashboard is intentionally designed to avoid overwhelming them. If they have no active cases linked to their identity, the interface is entirely neutral — calm, informative, and uncluttered. If they are involved in one or more active cases, the dashboard provides a clear, immediate summary of what requires their attention.

This design reflects a trauma-informed approach to interface design. During emotionally difficult situations, cognitive overload is a real risk. The dashboard prioritises clarity over density, showing only the most essential information: active cases, upcoming deadlines, and pending actions. Everything else is accessible through clearly labelled navigation, available when the user is ready to explore it.

## 6. The Committee Dashboard

### Purpose & Philosophy

The Committee Dashboard is the operational nerve centre of the SafeSpace UG ecosystem. While the other portals are designed around emotional safety and user accessibility, the Committee Dashboard is designed around **institutional accountability, operational efficiency, and procedural rigour.**

This is where designated committee members — appointed by the university to oversee the harassment response process — coordinate investigations, manage case workflows, enforce policy-mandated deadlines, schedule and conduct hearings, record formal decisions, and maintain a comprehensive audit trail of every action taken.

The interface is intentionally more data-oriented than the public-facing portals. Committee members are trained professionals who need rapid access to operational information: how many active cases require attention, which deadlines are approaching, which complaints have not yet been acknowledged, and which hearings need to be scheduled. The design prioritises operational clarity and efficiency while maintaining the gravity appropriate to the subject matter.

### The Committee Home — Operational Overview

Upon logging in, committee members are presented with a dashboard that provides an immediate snapshot of the current operational state. Active case counts, pending actions, approaching deadlines, and recent activity are all visible at a glance. This allows committee members to quickly assess their priorities and direct their attention where it is most urgently needed.

Deadline enforcement is a core feature of this dashboard. The university's policy mandates specific timeframes for every stage of the process — acknowledgement of complaints, notification of respondents, completion of investigations, and delivery of decisions. The dashboard visually flags items that are approaching their deadlines and highlights items that have exceeded them, ensuring that institutional obligations are met consistently.

## 7. Access Control & Participant Privacy

SafeSpace UG implements a carefully layered access model that reflects the distinct roles defined in the university's policy framework:

**Community Members & Visitors** can access the Public Awareness Platform freely, without any account or identification. They can also submit reports through the Reporting Portal — including anonymously — ensuring that the barrier to reporting is as low as possible.

**Complainants, Respondents & Witnesses** access the Participation Portal using their verified university credentials. Once authenticated, they can only see information directly relevant to cases in which they have an assigned role. A respondent cannot see a complainant's private communications. A witness cannot access hearing outcomes for cases they were not involved in. This strict role-based visibility protects all participants and maintains the integrity of the process.

**Committee Members** access the administrative Dashboard using their staff credentials and designated committee role. They have the operational visibility needed to manage cases effectively, but their actions are fully logged and auditable, ensuring that administrative power is exercised transparently and responsibly.

## 8. Evidence & Document Handling

When participants upload evidence or supporting documents through the platform — whether during the initial reporting process or later during case participation — those files are stored securely using encrypted, access-controlled storage. Documents are never publicly accessible. Access is granted only to authorised participants for limited timeframes, and every access event is recorded.

The platform handles this complexity invisibly. From the user's perspective, uploading a file is as simple as selecting it from their device. The security measures that protect that file from the moment of upload are entirely behind the scenes, requiring no technical knowledge from the user.

## 9. Confidentiality & Safety Measures

The platform implements comprehensive confidentiality protections throughout every layer of the system:

- **Personal information** is carefully controlled, with access limited strictly to those with a legitimate need to know within the context of a specific case.
- **Anonymous submissions** are genuinely anonymous — no identifying information is associated with the report in the platform's records.
- **Communications** between participants and the committee are conducted through the platform's secure messaging system, creating a documented record while preventing unauthorised access.
- **All administrative actions** taken by committee members are permanently recorded, creating an audit trail that supports institutional accountability and protects against procedural irregularities.

## 10. Institutional Readiness

SafeSpace UG has been designed for real-world institutional deployment. The platform's multi-application architecture provides natural security boundaries between different user groups. The consistent visual design across all applications creates a unified institutional identity while respecting the distinct needs of each audience.

The system is built to scale with the university's needs, supporting concurrent users across all portals while maintaining the responsiveness and reliability that a sensitive reporting platform demands. The unified aesthetic — consistent colours, typography, and interaction patterns across all applications — ensures that users feel they are engaging with a single, cohesive institutional service regardless of which portal they are using.

## 11. Summary & Institutional Value

**SafeSpace UG** represents the University of Ghana's commitment to translating policy into practice. The platform transforms the complex frameworks of the 2017 Sexual Harassment and Misconduct Policy into an accessible, navigable, and humane digital experience that serves every member of the university community.

For **students, faculty, and staff** seeking to understand their rights, the Public Awareness Platform provides clear, accessible education without requiring any personal information.

For **individuals who experience harassment**, the Reporting Portal offers a guided, supportive submission process that respects their pace, protects their identity if they choose anonymity, and ensures their report receives prompt institutional attention.

For **all participants in the resolution process**, the Participation Portal provides transparency, timeline visibility, and secure communication that reduces anxiety and supports informed participation.

For **the institution itself**, the Committee Dashboard ensures that investigations are conducted within mandated timeframes, decisions are properly documented, and the entire process maintains the auditability and accountability that institutional governance demands.

Together, these four applications form a comprehensive ecosystem that makes the university's harassment response framework not just a policy on paper, but a living, operational system that serves its community with care, rigour, and respect.

**SafeSpace UG — Because every member of the university community deserves to feel safe.**
